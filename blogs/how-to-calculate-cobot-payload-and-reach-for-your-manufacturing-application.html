How to Calculate Cobot Payload and Reach for Your Manufacturing Application

  • event_available 24-11-2026

If you think choosing a cobot is all about finding one with the longest reach or the capacity to lift heavy objects, you have to think better. Because the right cobot isn’t necessarily the strongest one; rather, it’s the one that can handle your specific manufacturing requirements. This is where payload and reach become relevant.

You may be planning to automate machine tending, assembly, or material handling; in any case, these two factors have to be considered so that you choose a cobot that works for you.

Wait, so how do you calculate the payload and reach that matches your task? Let’s find out.

What Is a Cobot Payload and What Does It Include?

A cobot payload is the maximum load a cobot can carry while performing a task. However, the payload isn’t just the weight of the object the cobots carry; it includes everything attached to the cobot’s wrist, such as the gripper, workpiece, additional tooling or sensors, and the tool changers. And the good news is it doesn’t end here. Centre of gravity, wrist movements, and loading conditions are also crucial to decide whether the cobot is safe to handle.

How to Calculate the Required Cobot Payload

The math is quite straightforward. You identify the heaviest component the cobot will handle and then add the weight of the tooling, gripper and other accessories to it. The sum total you get is the actual payload capacity.

Let’s say your facility needs a cobot to pick up metal components:

The calculation goes like this: Required payload = Workpiece + Gripper + Tooling

In the above example, the cobot needs to carry a total load of 6 kg. But that doesn’t mean that any cobots with a 6kg payload will suffice. You have to take physics into account as well. Yes, the centre of gravity. If the gripper or the product extends too far from the robot’s wrist, it creates additional force on the joints. Apart from this, the robot should also handle the force created while accelerating, stopping and changing direction.

Therefore, make sure to check the manufacturer's rated payload, centre of gravity, inertia and other specifications.

How to Calculate Cobot Reach for Your Application

Cobot reach refers to how far the robot arm can extend from the mounting position. The reachable workspace is the overall area the robot can access through its permitted movement.

To calculate the reach, you have to look into these factors:

  • Where the cobot is installed.
  • Where it needs to pick up products.
  • Where it needs to place them.
  • Whether it needs to reach around equipment or reach the workstation from a particular angle.

You have to measure the distance between the robot’s mounting position and the working locations. Then check whether the cobot can reach these positions with the required tools.

Additionally, you should also consider the gripper’s length and the approach angle. The cobot might be able to reach a particular point, but the gripper should also be positioned properly to pick up and place the object.

Why Payload and Reach Must Be Calculated Together

Take a cobot handling a heavy metal component with a long gripper, for instance. Even if the combined weight falls with the rated payload, the extended load may exceed the permitted wrist movement. This is why checking both the payload and the arm reach is important. Because the cobot should be able to handle the load throughout the movement and not just at a specific position.

Other Factors to Consider While Choosing a Cobot

While payload and reach are important, you can’t choose a cobot based on these alone. Consider the factors given below as well:

  • Repeatability: Can the cobot return to the same position consistently?
  • Cycle time: Can it complete the required movements quickly to meet the production goals.
  • Tool compatibility: Does it support the gripper, sensors, and other tools needed for the application?
  • Workspace: Is there enough space for the robot, machinery, operators and the surrounding equipment?
  • Safety: Has the whole cobot system as a whole been tested for potential risk?
  • Integration: Can the cobot communicate with the existing equipment?

How to Choose the Right Cobot for Your Manufacturing Application

Before selecting a collaborative robot, you have to have a clear idea about the production requirements. It is better if you keep a checklist:

  • Identify the task that you want to automate, such as machine tending, material handling, or assembly.
  • Calculate the maximum workpiece weight.
  • Make sure to include the gripper, sensors, tooling and other accessories while calculating the payload.
  • Identify the pick-up and place locations.
  • Look at the cycle time and repeatability requirements.
  • Check the available space, integration, and safety requirements.
  • Compare your findings with technical details provided by the manufacturers.

Conclusion

In short, choosing the right collaborative robot goes beyond just comparing the payload rating and arm reach. You need a solution that meets your actual requirements.
